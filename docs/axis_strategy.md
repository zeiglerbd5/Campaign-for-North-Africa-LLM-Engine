# Axis Strategy — Operation Compass

## Situation

The Italian 10th Army holds a forward line at Sidi Barrani (B3010) with 54 SP across 10 units. The British Western Desert Force fields 70 SP with vastly superior armor (30 SP at BAR 2-3 vs your 6 SP at BAR 1). You are outgunned, outarmored, and your infantry walks. Your one advantage: initiative rating 5 vs Allied 3 through GT42, meaning you usually move first.

## Objective

Survive until German reinforcements arrive (GT20-27). Trade space for time, preserve strength points, and hold the western VP hexes (Bardia 3 VP, Tobruk 5 VP, Derna 3 VP, Benghazi 5 VP = 16 VP). Every Axis unit that lives to see the DAK arrive is worth more than any hex east of Sollum.

## Phase 1: Fighting Withdrawal (GT1-8)

Do not hold Sidi Barrani. The 3 VP it offers is not worth the army.

Pull back immediately toward the Sollum escarpment (B3610/B3810). Move non-motorized infantry first — they are slowest and must start early. Maletti Group and the artillery group screen the retreat, using their motorization to stay one step ahead of British armor.

Priority order of march:
1. 2nd Libyan Division (farthest forward non-motorized units) — move GT1
2. 1st Libyan Division — move GT1-2, abandon Sidi Barrani
3. Maletti Group — rearguard, withdraws last

Use `break_contact` and `break_engaged` if caught. Do not close assault. A fire barrage from the artillery group at pursuing units is acceptable if it costs only 1 CPA and doesn't slow the retreat.

## Phase 2: Escarpment Defense (GT8-20)

Anchor on the Sollum escarpment (B3810) and Bardia (B4010). Escarpment terrain gives +1L defense shift on barrage and anti-armor — your weak M11 tanks and infantry survive longer here. The rough terrain at B3412 is also viable if the retreat stalls.

Stack infantry battalions in the escarpment hex. Place the artillery group one hex behind to provide barrage support. Maletti Group sits in reserve at Bardia, ready to counterattack any penetration or screen a further withdrawal to Tobruk.

Do not attack. The CRT punishes close assault at even odds — a 0:0 differential still risks D1/D2 results for the attacker. With BAR 1 tanks against BAR 3, anti-armor fire shreds the M11s. Hold, barrage, and let the British come to you where terrain shifts work in your favor.

Supply: Draw from Bardia port (510 tonnage/GT). Establish a dump at the escarpment. Axis evaporation is only 3% vs Allied 9% — your water lasts three times longer. This is a real advantage in a long defense.

## Phase 3: Consolidation (GT20-27)

German reconnaissance elements arrive around GT20, with 5th Light Division combat units by GT27. Once DAK arrives, the strategic picture inverts. Preserve Tobruk and Benghazi at all costs — these ports feed the combined Axis army.

Begin planning a counteroffensive only when combined Axis-German strength exceeds Allied strength at the point of contact. Until then, hold.

## Rules of Engagement

- **Never close assault below 2:1 ratio.** The CRT is brutal to attackers at even odds.
- **Never attack with the M11 tanks into British armor.** BAR 1 vs BAR 3 means anti-armor fire destroys you before close assault resolves.
- **Always retreat rather than lose a unit.** 0.5 VP per SP destroyed means losing a 10 SP battalion costs 5 VP — same as losing Tobruk.
- **Use barrage as your offensive tool.** Artillery at range doesn't risk destruction and benefits from escarpment terrain shifts.
- **Move first, shoot second.** Your initiative advantage means you can reposition before the British respond. Use it to avoid unfavorable engagements, not to charge into them.

## Victory Condition

Hold Tobruk (5 VP) + Benghazi (5 VP) + Bardia (3 VP) + Derna (3 VP) = 16 VP. Concede Sidi Barrani (3 VP) and Sollum (2 VP) if necessary. Destroy as few Allied units as possible by *not provoking fights you'll lose* — but make them pay for every hex they take. A marginal Axis victory at 5+ VP margin is achievable through defense alone.
